The Name

Mansio

Latin for dwelling or way-station. In the Roman world, a mansio was an official stopping place along the road—a place of rest that often became the seed of a permanent settlement.

The name carries the sense of inhabitation you seek, yet with texture and history. It is distinctive, pronounceable in both English and Spanish, and searchable. It does not sound like a contemporary brand. It sounds like something discovered.

The abbeys may be called Mansiones—stations and dwellings. The communities that grow around them are settlements formed by the life of the gospel.

In My Father’s house are many mansions; if it were not so, I would have told you. I go to prepare a place for you… I am the way, the truth, and the life. — John 14:2–6

Why “Living”

Gospel Living

“Living” connects directly to the Ordo vitae—an order of life, not a program of impact. It signals embodiment over activity. The Celtic abbey model, the minster communities, the parallel economic structures: these are about being a particular kind of people in a particular kind of place.

It is timeless rather than contemporary. “Impact” already feels dated. “Gospel Living” could have been spoken in 1824 or 2024 and meant the same thing. If we are building for 150 years, the name must survive the era that produced it.

  • Matches the Ordo vitae It names the formative document’s central concept.
  • Embodiment, not campaign It describes what the society is, not what it hopes to do to something else.
  • Works in every season Whether the surrounding church is healthy, declining, or gone.
  • Generational Equally valid in the first generation and the tenth.

The Pattern

Ordo Vitae

The Ordo salutis describes what God does in salvation. The Ordo vitae describes what the saved life looks like in practice.

Protestantism has a strong soteriology and a weakly developed account of the lived pattern that corresponds to it. Roman Catholicism has religious orders with structured rules of life. Eastern Orthodoxy has the liturgical cycle and hesychasm. Protestantism has largely relied on quiet times and small groups.

An Ordo vitae that maps formative practices, communal structures, and economic life onto the Ordo salutis gives communities something Protestants typically lack: a disciplined pattern of life that is theologically grounded rather than merely practically convenient.

This is not a renewal program for American evangelicalism. It is the building of structures that will outlast it. “Gospel Living” names a permanent posture.

The Way

Many Mansions

“In My Father’s house are many mansions; if it were not so, I would have told you. I go to prepare a place for you. And if I go and prepare a place for you, I will come again and receive you to Myself; that where I am, there you may be also. And where I go you know, and the way you know.”

Thomas said to Him, “Lord, we do not know where You are going, and how can we know the way?”

Jesus said to him, “I am the way, the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through Me.”

John 14:2–6

The Mansio is a way-station on the road. The ultimate dwelling is prepared by Christ Himself. We live now as those who know the Way.

A place along the way

The Mansio Society exists to form people and places ordered by the gospel—settlements of Gospel Living that can endure across generations.
